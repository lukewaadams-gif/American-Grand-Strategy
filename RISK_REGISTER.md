# Risk register

**Scales:**
- **L** is likelihood and **I** is impact, each scored 1-5.
- **Score** = L × I.
- **Trigger:** the observable signal that the risk is materialising.

**Owner roles:** GD = game director, TA = technical architect, PR = producer, LG = legal counsel.

| ID | Risk | L | I | Score | Mitigation | Trigger | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R-01 | **Scope gravity.** Three mature games (48k lines and hundreds of features) pull the team into porting everything. | 5 | 5 | **25** | `FEATURE_PRIORITY.md` is the contract, and new scope needs a cut of equal size. "Port models, not code." MVP feature freeze at M6. | The backlog grows faster than it closes; a feature is added "because the code exists". | PR |
| R-02 | **Real-person legal exposure.** BG generates invented scandals (harassment, hospitalisation) naming real politicians. RD can impeach and remove a real president. Ratings of living people are a related risk. | 4 | 5 | **20** | Fictional cast in every shipped build. No real names in event text, a rule enforced by a lint over the content files. Legal review before Early Access. Any future real-person mod has to sit behind clear terms. **Since the vertical slice:** the real roster (BG's 39) is opt-in, never the default, and carried in the save; a real player candidate never gets the two personal-scandal events, which campaign-level stand-ins replace (tested in `CampaignLayerTest`). | Any real name in shipped content; a platform (Steam) content query. | LG |
| R-03 | **Political fairness and review bombing.** The game can be perceived as favouring a party, a real figure or an ideology. | 4 | 4 | 16 | Toss-up default, with balance gates per party. A bipartisan content review of issue ownership, bill effects and event framing. Both agendas must be able to produce a good economy. Batch reports are published in the devlog. | Playtest sentiment split by player party; reviews mention "bias". | GD |
| R-04 | **Degenerate balance.** Examples so far: BG's Democrat won 40 of 40 general-election batch games, and RD approval stays between 45 and 50 whatever you do. The foundation's own first pass gave 100% re-election until fixed. | 4 | 4 | 16 | The batch tool and `BalanceTest` in CI, with gates: party win rate 45-55%, re-election 50-70%, war in 5-15% of terms. AutoPilot variants act as "strategy probes" to detect dominant strategies. | A gate fails; one AutoPilot variant beats the baseline by more than 10 points. | GD |
| R-05 | **Middle-of-term tedium.** Governing months 8-20 lack the climax of a campaign. | 4 | 5 | **20** | Scheduled set-pieces (budget, debt limit, State of the Union, Supreme Court), the Taiwan ladder, the midterm, and agenda pressure. A playtest of Act II **before** M3 depth work. Fallback: shorten governing to bi-monthly turns. | Playtesters skip turns, or session length drops in the 2029-30 stretch. | GD |
| R-06 | **Losing 2028 ends the run.** Ninety minutes of play followed by game over. | 3 | 3 | 9 | Fast restart with the same or a new seed; a short campaign (16 weeks); an Act I legacy score. Test an "opposition leader" coda for 1.0 if needed. **Since the vertical slice:** a defeat shows a post-mortem (the path to 270, the polling miss revealed, unspent money, the ground war) and offers a replay with the same seed. | Playtesters quit after a loss without restarting. | GD |
| R-07 | **Data licensing.** BG uses tonmcg county results (unverified licence), Nielsen DMAs (proprietary), and real podcasts and outlets (trademarks). | 3 | 4 | 12 | Drop county night and DMAs. Use MEDSL and Census data (CC0 or public domain) and fictional outlets. Keep a data provenance file in the repository. | Any dataset without a licence entry. | LG |
| R-08 | **War content sensitivity.** A PRC invasion of Taiwan with real hulls, decapitation strikes, a nuclear button and a "2026 Iran war" premise. Possible store or regional issues, and press framing. | 3 | 4 | 12 | Base game: abstract war decisions, no nuclear use, no named strikes on leaders. Command Mode (EXP-1) gets its own content review and can use generic unit names. Position the game as a strategy simulation with a "fiction" notice. | Store review feedback; press coverage. | PR |
| R-09 | **Content aging.** A 2028 game whose facts (2026 midterm results, leaders, calendars) are written in late 2026 and ship in 2027-28. | 4 | 3 | 12 | Fictional people. Facts sit in data files (the Senate classes and 2024 baseline are stable). Post-launch data patches. Avoid statements about real events after 2026. | Real-world events contradict the setup. | PR |
| R-10 | **Key-person dependency.** One author wrote all three source games. | 4 | 4 | 16 | Documented architecture, tests and contribution rules; module ownership; no single-file code. | Only one person can change a module. | PR |
| R-11 | **UI technology choice.** JavaFX could be weaker than expected for maps and animation, or for Steam Deck. | 3 | 3 | 9 | A 2-week spike at M4 with libGDX as fallback (ADR-2). The core is UI-agnostic either way. **Since the interface work:** the slice's interface is Swing with its own kit (ADR-7). It only reads the core and sends commands, so the M4 decision can still replace it without touching the simulation. | The spike misses frame-rate or controller targets. | TA |
| R-12 | **Simulation credibility vs. fun.** A strict model can be dull; a fun one can be called "unrealistic" by the core audience. | 3 | 4 | 12 | Legible formulas documented in game. Calibrate to real anchors (2024 results, Okun, CSIS war costs) and then tune for fun with sliders exposed. Difficulty modes. | Reviews split between "too random" and "too predictable". | GD |
| R-13 | **AI quality.** The campaign AI is heuristic. It may be exploitable (e.g. always targeting the same states) or seem dumb. | 3 | 3 | 9 | The AI reads the same polls the player sees and is deliberately learnable. Add strategy variants by difficulty; run AI-vs-AI batches per variant. | Players find a repeatable exploit. | TA |
| R-14 | **The theater model under-delivers.** The abstract war feels like a coin flip; or calibration against IslandChain proves impossible (IC invasions are decided by landing region alone). | 3 | 3 | 9 | Fix the IC region imbalance before using it as an oracle. Show players the factors and odds before the decision. EXP-1 is optional. | Testers can't explain why the war went as it did. | GD |
| R-15 | **Packaging and distribution.** JRE bundling size, macOS notarization, Linux variants. | 2 | 3 | 6 | `jpackage` with a jlink-trimmed runtime; a CI build matrix from M4. | A platform build fails late. | TA |
| R-16 | **Save compatibility across updates.** A command log replays with new rules, so the outcome can change after a patch. | 3 | 3 | 9 | Saves store the rules version. On mismatch, load from the 1.0 JSON snapshot, not by replay. A deprecation policy for commands. | A patch changes the result of a mid-game save. | TA |
| R-17 | **Localization.** Text-heavy content with US-specific concepts. | 2 | 2 | 4 | String keys from M2; English only at 1.0; a glossary. | Demand for localization from wishlists by region. | PR |
| R-18 | **Licence ambiguity of the source code.** The repositories have no LICENSE. | 2 | 4 | 8 | The owner confirms authorship and assigns rights to the new project in writing. The new repository is proprietary until decided. | A contributor or a fork asks. | LG |
| R-19 | **Source drift.** The source repositories keep changing after the audit. Resolute Desk moved to a "Version 2" (`2ce2b37`: 216 files, about 27,900 lines) after the audited `3fdbc7a`. | 4 | 2 | 8 | Ports cite the audited SHA; `docs/SOURCE_AUDIT.md` records upstream moves; audit a new version before porting from it (B-54). | An upstream HEAD differs from the audited SHA. | TA |
| R-20 | **The interface has not met players.** The desktop screens pass automated checks and were inspected rendered, but nobody has played them with a mouse on a real monitor, used a screen reader on them, or reviewed the visual design. Clarity, hierarchy and copy are unproven. | 4 | 3 | 12 | The human-review list in UI_DESIGN.md §15. A 5-player unaided usability test, a screen-reader pass on Windows and macOS, and a visual design review before calling the interface finished (B-55, B-56). | Testers miss the next step, ask what a number means, or quit at the first decision. | UX |
